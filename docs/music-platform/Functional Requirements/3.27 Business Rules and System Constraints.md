### 3.27 Business Rules and System Constraints

The Business Rules and System Constraints module shall define the operational rules, permission boundaries, data constraints, interaction rules, and functional limitations that govern the Social Music and Community Platform.

Business rules shall be enforced primarily on the backend. Frontend restrictions may improve user experience but shall not be considered sufficient for authorization or data protection.

#### 3.27.1 Business Rule Objectives

The business rules shall ensure that:

* Users can only perform authorized actions.
* Resources have clearly defined ownership.
* Community and group permissions are enforced consistently.
* Jam sessions maintain valid playback state.
* Messages maintain conversation integrity.
* External music providers are used according to their restrictions.
* Deleted or restricted resources are handled consistently.
* System limits prevent abuse and excessive resource consumption.
* Data remains consistent across multiple clients.

#### 3.27.2 User Account Rules

1. Each user shall have a unique system identifier.
2. A username, where required, shall be unique.
3. Required registration information shall be validated.
4. Passwords shall be securely hashed.
5. Suspended users shall have restricted access according to account policy.
6. Deleted accounts shall not be treated as active accounts.
7. Deactivated accounts shall not be able to perform restricted actions.
8. Authentication shall be required for protected resources.
9. Account ownership shall be determined by the authenticated user identity.
10. Users shall not be allowed to modify another user's account information without appropriate administrative permission.

#### 3.27.3 User Profile Rules

1. A user shall control their own profile information.
2. Profile visibility shall follow the user's privacy settings.
3. Profile images shall comply with configured file restrictions.
4. Username changes shall follow configured uniqueness and frequency rules.
5. Users shall not modify another user's profile without authorization.
6. Profile search shall respect discoverability settings.
7. Blocked users shall have restricted interactions according to blocking rules.
8. Deleted account information shall not remain unnecessarily discoverable.

#### 3.27.4 Authentication Rules

1. Protected API resources shall require authentication.
2. Authentication credentials shall not be exposed to other users.
3. Failed authentication attempts may be rate limited.
4. Password reset operations shall require a valid recovery mechanism.
5. Session revocation shall invalidate the affected session.
6. Logging out from one device shall not automatically terminate other sessions unless configured to do so.
7. A "logout all devices" operation shall revoke applicable active sessions.
8. Authentication tokens shall not be stored in publicly accessible server logs.

#### 3.27.5 Authorization Rules

Authorization shall be based on the authenticated user and the requested resource.

The system shall distinguish between:

```text id="y8q3m6"
Authentication
    ↓
Who is the user?

Authorization
    ↓
What is the user allowed to do?
```

A user being authenticated shall not automatically grant access to all resources.

#### 3.27.6 Resource Ownership Rules

Resources may have owners.

Examples include:

* User profile
* Conversation
* Group
* Community
* Channel
* Jam

Ownership shall determine which administrative operations are available.

Ownership shall not prevent other users from accessing a resource when explicit membership or permissions grant access.

#### 3.27.7 Direct Conversation Rules

1. A direct conversation shall contain the appropriate participants.
2. A user shall only access conversations they are authorized to access.
3. Messages shall belong to a valid conversation.
4. A user shall not send messages to a conversation they cannot access.
5. Conversation membership changes shall be validated by the backend.
6. Deleted or blocked users shall follow the applicable messaging policy.

#### 3.27.8 Message Rules

1. A message shall have an authenticated sender.
2. A message shall belong to a valid conversation, group, or channel.
3. Message content shall satisfy configured validation rules.
4. Empty messages shall not be submitted unless the message type explicitly permits it.
5. Attachments shall be validated before association with a message.
6. Users shall only edit messages they are authorized to edit.
7. Users shall only delete messages they are authorized to delete.
8. Moderators and administrators may have additional content-management permissions.
9. Deleted messages shall follow the configured deletion policy.
10. Message timestamps shall be generated or validated by the server where required.

#### 3.27.9 Message Editing Rules

The system may allow message editing subject to configured restrictions.

Possible rules include:

* Only the sender may edit a message.
* Editing may be limited to a configured time period.
* Deleted messages cannot be edited.
* Moderated or locked messages cannot be edited.
* The system may retain an edit indicator.
* Audit records may be generated for administrative changes.

#### 3.27.10 Message Deletion Rules

Message deletion shall distinguish between:

```text id="z4m8p2"
User Deletion
Moderator Deletion
Administrator Deletion
System Deletion
```

The resulting visible state shall follow the system's configured retention and moderation policy.

#### 3.27.11 Message Reaction Rules

1. A user may add a reaction only to a message they can access.
2. A user should not create duplicate identical reactions when the system permits only one per emoji/type.
3. Removing a reaction shall only affect the authenticated user's applicable reaction.
4. Reaction counts shall remain consistent with stored reaction records.
5. Reaction updates shall be delivered to authorized clients.

#### 3.27.12 Thread Rules

1. A thread reply shall reference a valid parent message.
2. The parent message must belong to the same conversation or channel context.
3. A user shall only access a thread if they can access its parent conversation.
4. Thread replies shall follow the same message validation rules.
5. Deleting a parent message shall follow a defined thread-retention policy.
6. Thread counts shall reflect valid replies.

#### 3.27.13 Mention Rules

1. Mentions shall reference valid users.
2. A user should only be mentionable where they have access to the relevant conversation or community context.
3. Mention notifications shall only be delivered to authorized recipients.
4. Blocked or restricted relationships may affect mention behavior.
5. Mention rendering shall not expose private user information.

#### 3.27.14 Group Conversation Rules

1. A group shall have an identifiable owner or administrative structure.
2. Group membership shall be stored explicitly.
3. Only authorized users may add members.
4. Members may leave a group unless restricted by a defined system rule.
5. Administrative roles shall determine member-management permissions.
6. A group cannot be accessed by a user who is not an authorized member.
7. Group limits shall be configurable.
8. Group changes shall be reflected in realtime where applicable.

#### 3.27.15 Group Role Rules

A group may define roles such as:

```text id="q6n4r8"
Owner
Administrator
Member
```

Permissions may include:

| Action                 | Owner | Administrator |       Member |
| ---------------------- | ----: | ------------: | -----------: |
| Send messages          |   Yes |           Yes |          Yes |
| Add members            |   Yes |  Configurable | Configurable |
| Remove members         |   Yes |  Configurable |           No |
| Edit group information |   Yes |  Configurable |           No |
| Change roles           |   Yes |       Limited |           No |
| Delete group           |   Yes |            No |           No |

The exact permission matrix shall be configurable according to the final product requirements.

#### 3.27.16 Community Rules

1. A community shall have an owner.
2. Community membership shall be explicitly represented.
3. Community access shall follow its configured visibility.
4. Community owners and administrators shall manage community settings according to their permissions.
5. Moderators shall only perform moderation actions granted to their role.
6. Members shall only access channels they are authorized to access.
7. Community membership changes shall be validated by the backend.
8. Community limits may be configured.

#### 3.27.17 Community Visibility

Communities may support visibility states such as:

```text id="m7q3n9"
Public
Private
Invite Only
```

The selected visibility shall determine discovery and membership behavior.

#### 3.27.18 Community Channel Rules

1. Each channel shall belong to a valid community.
2. Channel names shall be unique within the applicable community scope.
3. Private channels shall restrict access to authorized members.
4. Announcement channels may restrict message creation to authorized roles.
5. Deleted channels shall not accept new messages.
6. Channel membership shall follow community and channel permissions.

#### 3.27.19 Community Moderation Rules

Moderators and administrators may be allowed to:

* Remove messages.
* Manage members.
* Restrict members.
* Review reports.
* Manage channels.
* Apply moderation actions.

Moderation permissions shall be explicitly defined and shall not automatically provide unrestricted system administration access.

#### 3.27.20 Blocking Rules

The system may support user blocking.

When one user blocks another, applicable restrictions may include:

* Direct messaging
* Mentions
* Profile interaction
* Invitations
* Presence visibility

The exact blocking behavior shall be consistently applied across the application.

#### 3.27.21 Reporting Rules

Users may report:

* Users
* Messages
* Communities
* Channels
* Other supported content

A report shall contain sufficient information for authorized moderators or administrators to investigate.

Possible report states include:

```text id="k2r7m5"
Pending
Under Review
Resolved
Dismissed
Escalated
Duplicate
```

#### 3.27.22 Moderation Rules

Moderation actions shall require appropriate permissions.

Possible actions include:

* Warning
* Content removal
* Member restriction
* Member removal
* Temporary suspension
* Permanent account action where authorized

Moderation actions should be auditable.

#### 3.27.23 Music Rules

1. Music search shall use an approved provider.
2. Music playback shall follow provider capabilities.
3. Provider-specific restrictions shall be respected.
4. Protected music shall not be copied or redistributed without authorization.
5. The application shall not circumvent provider playback restrictions.
6. Music metadata shall only be stored to the extent permitted.
7. Provider rate limits shall be respected.
8. Provider failures shall not corrupt application data.

#### 3.27.24 Music Favorites and History

If favorites or history are implemented:

1. Users may manage their own saved music records.
2. Music history shall belong to the relevant user.
3. History visibility shall respect privacy settings.
4. Provider identifiers shall be stored together with provider information where necessary.
5. Deleted provider content shall be handled according to provider availability.

#### 3.27.25 Jam Creation Rules

1. A Jam shall have a host.
2. The host must be an authenticated user.
3. A Jam shall have a unique identifier.
4. The host shall define the Jam's privacy configuration.
5. The Jam may have a participant limit.
6. A Jam shall have an active/inactive lifecycle.
7. The host shall be authorized to control the Jam.

#### 3.27.26 Jam Privacy Rules

A Jam may support:

```text id="g8m2v6"
Public
Community Only
Invite Only
Private
```

Access shall be validated according to the selected visibility.

#### 3.27.27 Jam Membership Rules

1. A participant must satisfy the Jam's access requirements.
2. A user cannot join a private Jam without authorization.
3. A participant should only have one active membership record per Jam.
4. Leaving a Jam shall remove or deactivate the participant membership according to the data-retention policy.
5. Participant presence shall be synchronized through the realtime system.

#### 3.27.28 Jam Host Rules

The host may be authorized to:

* Play
* Pause
* Seek
* Change tracks
* Manage participants
* Change Jam settings
* End the Jam

The host shall not gain unrestricted system-administrator privileges through ownership of a Jam.

#### 3.27.29 Jam Playback Rules

The Jam playback state shall contain sufficient information to synchronize participants.

A conceptual state may include:

```json id="y6h3t8"
{
  "track_id": "track-123",
  "status": "playing",
  "position": 42.35,
  "server_timestamp": 1790000000000,
  "state_version": 18
}
```

Clients shall use the server-provided state to calculate the expected playback position.

#### 3.27.30 Jam Synchronization Rules

1. Playback state changes shall be generated by authorized users.
2. State changes shall include a server timestamp where required.
3. Clients shall reconcile local playback with the authoritative Jam state.
4. Repeated events shall not create inconsistent playback state.
5. Clients reconnecting to an active Jam shall retrieve the latest state.
6. Minor playback drift may be corrected periodically.
7. Realtime transport shall synchronize state rather than transmit the protected music audio.

#### 3.27.31 Jam State Ordering

Jam state changes shall use an ordering mechanism such as:

* State version
* Server timestamp
* Event sequence number

Example:

```text id="c8r4m7"
State 17
   ↓
State 18
   ↓
State 19
```

A stale state should not overwrite a newer authoritative state.

#### 3.27.32 Jam Host Disconnection

If the host disconnects, the system shall follow a predefined policy.

Possible policies include:

```text id="r5m8q3"
Temporary Reconnection
        OR
Host Transfer
        OR
Jam Paused
        OR
Jam Ended
```

The final behavior shall be defined during implementation.

#### 3.27.33 Jam Ending Rules

When a Jam ends:

1. New participants shall not be allowed to join.
2. Playback synchronization shall stop.
3. Participants shall receive the appropriate realtime update.
4. Historical Jam information may be retained according to the data-retention policy.

#### 3.27.34 Notification Rules

Notifications shall only be delivered to users who are authorized to receive the related information.

Examples include:

* Message notifications
* Mention notifications
* Reaction notifications
* Community invitations
* Jam invitations
* Security notifications

Users shall be able to configure supported notification preferences.

#### 3.27.35 Notification Deduplication

The system should prevent unnecessary duplicate notifications.

For example, a user receiving multiple realtime events for the same underlying action should not receive multiple identical persistent notifications unless explicitly intended.

#### 3.27.36 File and Attachment Rules

1. Files shall be validated before storage.
2. File size shall comply with configured limits.
3. File type shall comply with supported formats.
4. Private files shall require authorization.
5. File metadata shall reference the owning resource.
6. Unused files should be removed according to cleanup rules.
7. Suspicious files may be blocked or scanned.
8. Original filenames should not be used directly as secure storage identifiers.

#### 3.27.37 File Ownership

Attachment access shall be determined by the resource to which the file belongs.

For example:

```text id="p7m4q2"
Message
   │
   └── Attachment
         │
         └── Conversation Access
```

A user authorized to access the message may access its attachment according to the applicable policy.

#### 3.27.38 Search Rules

Search results shall respect:

* Authentication
* Resource membership
* Privacy settings
* Blocking
* Community permissions
* Channel permissions
* Deleted content
* Visibility settings

The search system shall not expose inaccessible records merely because they exist in an index.

#### 3.27.39 Search Ranking

Search relevance may be determined by:

* Text relevance
* Match type
* Recency
* Resource type

The ranking implementation shall remain configurable.

Search ranking shall not override authorization restrictions.

#### 3.27.40 Notification and Privacy Rules

Users shall not receive notifications containing information they are no longer authorized to access.

If access to a resource changes, future notifications shall follow the updated permission state.

#### 3.27.41 Account Deletion Rules

Account deletion shall follow a defined lifecycle.

A possible lifecycle is:

```text id="s8q2m5"
Active
  ↓
Deletion Requested
  ↓
Grace Period
  ↓
Deleted
  ↓
Retention / Anonymization
```

The exact retention period shall be defined by system policy.

#### 3.27.42 Deleted User Data

Account deletion shall determine how related resources are handled.

Possible behaviors include:

* Anonymizing authored messages.
* Removing profile information.
* Removing private user settings.
* Deleting personal attachments where applicable.
* Retaining records required for moderation, security, legal, or operational reasons.

The final policy shall be documented before production deployment.

#### 3.27.43 Data Retention Rules

Different data categories may have different retention periods.

Examples include:

```text
Messages
Attachments
Notifications
Audit Logs
Sessions
Temporary Files
Jam History
Search Indexes
```

Retention rules shall be configurable where practical.

#### 3.27.44 Audit Rules

Important security, administrative, and moderation actions shall be auditable.

Audit records shall identify the relevant actor and resource without storing unnecessary secrets or sensitive credentials.

#### 3.27.45 Rate Limiting Rules

The system shall apply rate limits to operations susceptible to abuse.

Potential targets include:

* Login
* Registration
* Password reset
* Message sending
* Search
* File uploads
* Music search
* Jam actions
* Invitations
* Reports

Rate limits shall be configurable.

#### 3.27.46 Duplicate Request Rules

State-changing operations should support idempotency where duplicate requests could cause problems.

Examples include:

* Sending a message
* Creating a Jam
* Joining a Jam
* Adding a reaction
* Processing a webhook

#### 3.27.47 Concurrency Rules

The system shall account for simultaneous operations from multiple clients.

Examples include:

```text id="d3m7p8"
Two devices editing the same message
Two users changing group membership
Host seeking while another state update arrives
Multiple clients adding reactions
```

The backend shall determine authoritative state.

#### 3.27.48 Realtime Rules

Realtime events shall be treated as synchronization mechanisms rather than the permanent source of truth.

The general principle shall be:

```text id="q8r3m1"
Database / Domain State
        ↓
Authoritative State

Realtime
        ↓
State Distribution
```

If a client misses an event, it should be able to recover the current state through the API.

#### 3.27.49 Realtime Provider Failure

If the primary realtime provider fails:

1. The client may attempt the configured fallback where supported.
2. The application shall continue using API operations where possible.
3. Persistent data shall remain available.
4. The client shall indicate degraded realtime status.
5. Duplicate event processing shall be prevented.

#### 3.27.50 API Consistency Rules

API endpoints shall use consistent behavior for:

* Authentication
* Authorization
* Validation
* Pagination
* Error responses
* Resource representation
* Status codes

This reduces differences between web, mobile, and desktop clients.

#### 3.27.51 Cross-Platform Rules

All clients shall use the same backend business rules.

```text id="m4q8s2"
                 Laravel API
                     │
          ┌──────────┼──────────┐
          ▼          ▼          ▼
         Web       Mobile     Desktop
          │          │          │
          └──── Same Business ──┘
                  Rules
```

A user should not receive greater authorization merely because they are using a different client.

#### 3.27.52 System Limits

The application shall define configurable limits for resource consumption.

Potential limits include:

* Message size
* File size
* Attachment count
* Group size
* Community size
* Jam participant count
* API requests
* Search requests
* Notification frequency
* Storage usage

These limits may be adjusted as system capacity changes.

#### 3.27.53 Performance Constraints

The system shall avoid unnecessary operations that may negatively affect performance.

Examples include:

* Excessive polling
* Repeated music provider requests
* High-frequency Jam state broadcasts
* Unnecessary presence events
* Loading excessive message history
* Sending large media files through realtime channels

#### 3.27.54 Jam State Frequency Constraint

Jam playback position shall not be broadcast continuously at very high frequency.

Instead, playback actions such as:

```text
Play
Pause
Seek
Track Change
```

shall be broadcast as state changes.

Clients shall calculate local playback position between synchronization points.

#### 3.27.55 Realtime Message Constraints

Typing and presence events should be throttled or debounced.

The system shall avoid generating an excessive number of realtime events from a single user interaction.

#### 3.27.56 External Provider Constraints

Third-party services shall remain subject to:

* API quotas
* Rate limits
* Service availability
* Provider policies
* Licensing
* Regional restrictions

The system shall not assume unlimited availability from an external provider.

#### 3.27.57 Hosting Constraints

The initial hosting environment may restrict:

* Long-running processes
* WebSocket servers
* Queue workers
* Scheduled workers
* Custom network ports
* Persistent background services

The system architecture shall account for these limitations.

External managed services may be used where appropriate.

#### 3.27.58 Security Constraints

The system shall:

* Use HTTPS in production.
* Protect authentication credentials.
* Validate user input.
* Enforce authorization server-side.
* Protect private realtime channels.
* Protect uploaded files.
* Avoid exposing secrets.
* Apply appropriate rate limits.
* Log important security events.
* Avoid storing unnecessary sensitive information.

#### 3.27.59 Data Integrity Constraints

The database shall maintain integrity through:

* Foreign keys
* Unique constraints
* Required fields
* Appropriate indexes
* Transactions
* Validation
* Referential integrity

Business-critical operations shall not depend solely on frontend validation.

#### 3.27.60 Transaction Rules

Operations involving multiple related records should use transactions when atomicity is required.

Example:

```text id="z5q8n3"
Create Group
   ↓
Create Group Record
   ↓
Create Owner Membership
   ↓
Create Initial Settings
   ↓
Commit
```

If a critical operation fails, the transaction should be rolled back where appropriate.

#### 3.27.61 Event Dispatch Rules

Realtime events representing database state should preferably be dispatched after the corresponding transaction has successfully committed.

This prevents clients from receiving an event for data that was ultimately rolled back.

#### 3.27.62 Background Processing Rules

Long-running operations should be handled asynchronously where appropriate.

Examples include:

* Email
* File processing
* Search indexing
* Notifications
* Cleanup
* External provider synchronization

Realtime playback synchronization shall not depend on slow background jobs.

#### 3.27.63 Business Rule Change Management

Business rules may evolve as the product develops.

Changes shall be:

* Documented
* Reviewed
* Tested
* Versioned where appropriate
* Reflected in API behavior
* Reflected in frontend behavior where necessary
