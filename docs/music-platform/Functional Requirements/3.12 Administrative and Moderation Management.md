### 3.12 Administrative and Moderation Management

The Administrative and Moderation Management module will provide authorized personnel with tools for managing the platform, communities, users, content, and security-related activities.

The system shall distinguish between **System Administration** and **Community Moderation**. System administrators will manage platform-wide resources, while community owners, administrators, and moderators will manage resources within their assigned communities.

Administrative privileges shall be enforced by the backend and shall not depend solely on frontend controls.

#### 3.12.1 System Administration

System administrators shall have access to authorized platform-level management functions.

Possible functions include:

* User management
* Account status management
* Community management
* Content management
* Moderation management
* Role management
* Security monitoring
* System configuration
* Audit-log review
* Platform statistics

Access to these functions shall be restricted to authorized system administrators.

#### 3.12.2 User Management

Authorized administrators shall be able to manage user accounts according to their permissions.

Possible operations include:

* View user information
* Search users
* Update account status
* Deactivate an account
* Reactivate an account
* Review reported users
* Review relevant account activity

Administrators shall not automatically have access to private user content unless the platform's documented policies and authorization model permit such access.

#### 3.12.3 Account Status

The system may support different account states.

Examples include:

```text id="4p7m2a"
Active
Inactive
Suspended
Pending Verification
Deleted
```

Account status shall affect authentication and access according to defined business rules.

For example, a suspended account may be prevented from logging into the platform while an administrator reviews the account.

#### 3.12.4 Role Management

The system shall support role-based administrative permissions.

Possible platform roles include:

* System Administrator
* Community Owner
* Community Administrator
* Moderator
* Regular User

A user may have different permissions depending on the scope of the resource.

For example:

```text id="k6v8x1"
User A
│
├── Community X → Administrator
│
├── Community Y → Moderator
│
└── Community Z → Member
```

This resource-based role model prevents global privileges from being unnecessarily assigned.

#### 3.12.5 Community Administration

Community owners and authorized administrators shall be able to manage their communities.

Possible functions include:

* Update community information
* Manage community members
* Create or remove channels
* Manage channel permissions
* Assign community roles
* Manage invitations
* Review reports
* Configure community settings

Community administrators shall only manage communities where they have appropriate permissions.

#### 3.12.6 Moderator Functions

Moderators may be given limited content-management permissions.

Possible functions include:

* Review reported content
* Remove inappropriate content
* Restrict users from specific community resources
* Issue warnings
* Manage channel-level moderation
* Review community activity

Moderator permissions shall be configurable and shall not automatically include system-level administrative functions.

#### 3.12.7 Content Reporting

The system may allow users to report content or accounts.

Reportable resources may include:

* Users
* Messages
* Communities
* Channels
* Attachments
* Other supported content

A report may contain:

* Report identifier
* Reporter
* Reported resource
* Reason
* Additional description
* Status
* Reviewer
* Creation timestamp
* Resolution timestamp

#### 3.12.8 Report Status

Reports may have defined workflow states.

For example:

```text id="p4x7q0"
Pending
   ↓
Under Review
   ↓
Resolved
```

Other states may include:

* Dismissed
* Escalated
* Duplicate

The final workflow shall be defined during implementation.

#### 3.12.9 Moderation Actions

Authorized moderators and administrators may perform documented actions based on their permissions.

Possible actions include:

* Remove content
* Restrict a user
* Suspend community access
* Remove a member
* Lock a channel
* Resolve a report
* Escalate a report

Moderation actions should be recorded when appropriate for accountability.

#### 3.12.10 Content Removal

When authorized personnel remove content, the system shall define how the content is represented.

Possible approaches include:

```text id="v2q5k8"
Original Message
       ↓
Content removed
       ↓
"Message removed by moderator"
```

The system may retain limited metadata for moderation and audit purposes according to its data-retention policy.

#### 3.12.11 Community Member Management

Authorized community administrators may manage community membership.

Possible operations include:

* Invite members
* Remove members
* Restrict members
* Assign roles
* Revoke roles
* Review membership status

All membership changes shall be subject to authorization rules.

#### 3.12.12 Channel Management

Authorized community personnel shall be able to manage channels.

Possible operations include:

* Create channel
* Rename channel
* Update description
* Change visibility
* Change channel permissions
* Archive channel
* Delete channel

Private channels shall maintain separate access-control rules.

#### 3.12.13 Community Invitations

Community administrators may invite users to communities.

The system may support:

* Direct invitations
* Invitation links
* Invitation expiration
* Invitation revocation
* Invitation status tracking

Private communities shall require appropriate authorization before an invitation can be created.

#### 3.12.14 Administrative Dashboard

The system shall provide an administrative interface for authorized administrators.

A dashboard may display:

* User statistics
* Community statistics
* Active users
* Active communities
* Report counts
* Moderation activity
* System events
* Other operational metrics

Administrative dashboard information shall only be available to authorized personnel.

#### 3.12.15 Community Dashboard

Community administrators may have a separate management interface.

The interface may provide:

* Member overview
* Channel management
* Community settings
* Role management
* Reports
* Moderation actions
* Community activity

Community administrators shall not automatically access platform-wide administration functions.

#### 3.12.16 Audit Logs

Administrative actions should be recorded where appropriate.

Possible audit events include:

* Role changes
* Account status changes
* Content removal
* Member removal
* Channel changes
* Community settings changes
* Moderation decisions
* Administrative configuration changes

An audit record may include:

```text id="f8s2w6"
actor_id
action
resource_type
resource_id
metadata
created_at
```

Sensitive information should not be unnecessarily stored in audit logs.

#### 3.12.17 Administrative Security

Administrative functions shall have stronger access controls than normal user functionality.

Security measures may include:

* Role-based authorization
* Resource-based policies
* Session security
* Rate limiting
* Audit logging
* Additional authentication factors in future versions
* Administrative activity monitoring

Administrative endpoints shall be protected at the API level.

#### 3.12.18 Moderation and Real-Time Updates

Moderation-related changes may be communicated to affected users through real-time events.

For example:

```text id="9d2j4x"
Moderator removes message
        │
        ▼
Database updated
        │
        ▼
MessageRemoved event
        │
        ▼
Connected clients
        │
        ▼
Message UI updated
```

The real-time event shall not expose unnecessary moderation information.

#### 3.12.19 Administrative Notifications

Administrators may receive notifications for relevant platform events.

Examples include:

* New report
* Escalated report
* Community issue
* Security-related event
* Account-management event

Community administrators shall only receive notifications related to resources they are authorized to manage.

#### 3.12.20 Moderation Permissions

The system shall use explicit permission definitions rather than assuming that all moderators have identical capabilities.

For example:

```text id="u7j5p3"
Community Administrator
├── Manage Members
├── Manage Channels
├── Manage Roles
└── Review Reports

Moderator
├── Review Reports
├── Moderate Messages
└── Restrict Members
```

The final permission matrix shall be defined during the authorization design phase.

#### 3.12.21 Administrative Search

Authorized personnel may search administrative resources.

Possible searches include:

* Users
* Communities
* Reports
* Messages
* Channels
* Moderation records

Administrative search shall remain subject to the administrator's permissions and applicable privacy rules.

#### 3.12.22 Platform Configuration

System administrators may manage selected platform configuration values.

Possible configuration areas include:

* Upload limits
* Feature availability
* Registration settings
* Community settings
* Notification settings
* Rate limits
* Moderation settings

Sensitive infrastructure configuration shall remain protected through server-side environment configuration rather than being exposed as ordinary application settings.

#### 3.12.23 Feature Control

The system may support feature flags for controlled feature rollout.

For example:

```text id="j2r9x5"
Feature: Jam
Status: Enabled

Feature: Voice Channels
Status: Disabled

Feature: Advanced Recommendations
Status: Experimental
```

Feature controls may be scoped globally or to selected user groups where appropriate.

#### 3.12.24 Data Access Restrictions

Administrative access shall follow the principle of least privilege.

A system administrator's ability to manage platform resources does not necessarily mean unrestricted access to all user-generated content.

The system shall define which administrative functions require access to specific user data and limit access accordingly.

#### 3.12.25 Moderation History

The system may maintain moderation history for accountability and operational review.

History may include:

* Action taken
* Actor
* Target resource
* Reason
* Timestamp
* Result

Moderation history shall be protected from unauthorized modification.
